# Masyu

> White pearls demand a straight pass-through, black pearls demand a sharp turn — one loop has to satisfy every one of them.

**At a glance** · Solo · one puzzle, one answer · 10–20 min · Play it under **Logic** (`/logic`).

## What it is

Masyu ("evil influence" in Japanese, though most players just call it "pearls") is a loop-drawing puzzle like Slitherlink, but the constraints sit on the cells themselves rather than the edges of a dot grid. Each pearl — white or black — imposes a local shape requirement on the loop as it passes through that cell: white pearls want the loop to slide straight through, black pearls want it to bend sharply. Reasoning outward from the pearls, cell by cell, is enough to pin the entire loop down uniquely, with no guessing required, even though at first glance a lone pearl seems to allow several shapes.

## Goal

Draw a single closed loop through cell centres so that it **passes through every pearl on the board**, obeying each pearl's shape rule, and touches no cell twice.

## The grid & clues

The board is a 6×6 grid of cells — the fixed size used throughout ChessRetabled, freshly regenerated every time you start a new puzzle. The loop is drawn between cell centres (not along the outer grid lines the way Slitherlink's is), so a 6×6 board has a 6×5 field of horizontal loop-segments and a 5×6 field of vertical ones. Some cells hold a **white pearl** (○), some hold a **black pearl** (●), and the rest are unmarked and impose no constraint at all. Boards are generated by growing a random loop shape and reading off which cells qualify as legal white or black pearls, then thinning pearls one at a time while a uniqueness solver confirms the puzzle still has exactly one answer.

## Rules

1. Draw a single closed loop through cell centres, using only horizontal and vertical steps between orthogonally adjacent cells.
2. The loop passes through **every** pearled cell, and through no cell more than once.
3. **White pearl:** the loop must go **straight through** that cell (horizontal or vertical) — but it must **turn** in at least one of the two cells immediately before or after it, along that same straight line.
4. **Black pearl:** the loop must **turn** in that cell (an L-shaped bend) — and then run **straight** for at least one full cell on **both** sides of that turn.
5. Unmarked cells the loop happens to pass through have no shape requirement — straight or turning is fine there.
6. The loop must be one single connected cycle, not several separate loops, and it may not cross or touch itself.

## How it works in ChessRetabled

- **Opening a puzzle.** Under **Logic**, choose Masyu. Each board is a fresh 6×6 grid: a random loop shape is grown first, its white and black pearls are read straight off that shape, and then pearls are removed one at a time only while a uniqueness check confirms the puzzle still has exactly one legal loop.
- **Drawing the loop.** Click the small buttons *between* cell centres to toggle a segment on or off — there's no ✕ state here, only "drawn" or "not drawn," since Masyu boards tend to need fewer scratch marks than Slitherlink's edge-elimination style.
- **The 💡 hint.** Every generated puzzle stores its full solution segment set, so a hint never has to re-solve anything. Press **Hint** for a *nudge* — one missing loop segment lights up; press again to *reveal*, drawing it in. Using a hint marks the puzzle **assisted** in your history and Dashboard.
- **Completion.** The instant your drawn segments form one closed loop obeying every pearl, the win is detected and logged to your archive automatically, with no separate submit step.

## Strategy

### Beginner — reading each pearl in isolation
- **Black pearls near the edge or corner are highly forced.** A black pearl one cell in from a border can only turn one of two ways (the other way would run the "straight" leg off the board), so check board-edge proximity before anything else.
- **Two adjacent pearls, either colour, restrict each other.** If a white and black pearl sit next to each other, the loop segment between them is shared, so work out that segment's direction from whichever pearl gives you a firmer constraint first, then carry the result to the other.
- **Draw both legs of a black pearl immediately.** As soon as you know which way a black pearl turns, you also know it must run straight for the *next* full cell on each leg — extend both arms one cell before moving on to the next pearl.
- **A white pearl on the border, one cell in, is forced to run parallel to that border.** Running the other direction would immediately hit the edge with no room left to turn on that side.
- **Unmarked cells next to the border have fewer legal shapes too.** Even without a pearl, a border-adjacent cell can't have a loop segment pointing off the board, which quietly removes options as you fill in neighbours.

### Intermediate — chaining pearls together
- **Two white pearls in a straight line, close together, often force the same line to persist between them.** If nothing else could explain a turn in between, the straight run must connect them directly.
- **A black pearl's turn direction can sometimes be deduced from a neighbouring white pearl's straight requirement**, since the segment leaving the white pearl's straight run has to arrive at the black pearl's cell from a specific side, pre-determining one of its two legs.
- **Track dead ends.** Every non-pearl cell the loop visits still needs exactly two loop-neighbours (in a straight line or a turn) — if a partially-drawn path would leave a cell with only one possible exit, that path is wrong and should be backed out.
- **Use the "every cell touched at most once" rule aggressively.** A candidate segment that would force the loop back into a cell it already used is illegal, even if it satisfies every pearl locally — this rules out surprisingly many otherwise-plausible extensions.
- **When two pearls are far apart with only unmarked cells between them, look for the shortest legal connecting path first.** Loops rarely wander without reason on a small board, so the direct route is usually — though not always — correct; use it as a hypothesis to test, not a certainty.

### Advanced — global loop consistency
- **The loop is the boundary of an inside/outside region**, exactly like Slitherlink but shifted onto the small faces between four cells rather than cell edges — a partially-solved board can often be completed faster by asking "is this face inside or outside?" for each face, rather than by tracing segments directly.
- **Look for forced completions late in the solve.** Once most of the board's segments are determined, the handful of undetermined cells are usually locked to a single legal shape by the "single connected loop, no branching, no premature closure" rule — check whether an early closure would strand pearls that still need visiting.
- **Count remaining pearls versus remaining loop length.** A loop that's nearly closed but still has unvisited pearls tells you your current path is wrong somewhere upstream, even if every segment drawn so far looks locally legal.
- **Use contradiction the same way as in Slitherlink.** If an ambiguous segment's two options both eventually strand a pearl or force a premature loop closure, you've proved the segment is illegal without knowing in advance which way it actually goes — invaluable on the last few undetermined cells.

## Traps & common mistakes

- **Confusing which side of a white pearl needs to turn.** The rule only requires a turn on *at least one* side, not both — don't force an unnecessary turn on the side that's actually meant to run straight further.
- **Forgetting the "at most once per cell" rule.** It's easy to draw a path that would technically satisfy every pearl but revisits a cell, which isn't a valid simple loop.
- **Treating a black pearl's straight legs as optional.** Both legs, one full cell each side, are mandatory — a black pearl with only one straight leg drawn is not yet fully constrained, even if it looks finished.
- **Second-guessing an edge pearl.** Pearls near the border have very limited legal shapes; players sometimes look for hidden flexibility that the geometry simply doesn't allow.
- **Drawing a segment that isolates part of the board.** A stray closed sub-loop that doesn't touch the main loop is a classic near-miss — always check the full loop is one connected cycle before declaring victory.

## A worked example

A black pearl one cell from the top-left corner:

```
 ● . . . . .
 . . . . . .     The black pearl at row 1 (0-indexed),
 . . . . . .     column 0 can't turn "up-then-left" —
 . . . . . .     there's no row above it. So it must
 . . . . . .     turn down-and-right, then run straight
 . . . . . .     one more cell in each of those directions.
```

The border alone rules out one of the two possible turn shapes before you look at any other clue — always check board-edge proximity for black pearls first.

## Glossary

- **White pearl (○)** — the loop runs straight through; it must turn in an adjacent cell on at least one side.
- **Black pearl (●)** — the loop turns here; it must run straight for a full cell on both sides.
- **Leg** — the straight run of loop segments extending from a black pearl's turn.
- **Segment** — one drawn edge of the loop between two adjacent cell centres.
